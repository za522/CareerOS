# CareerOS CV Formatting Rules

These rules govern imported CV cleanup, AI tailoring, the editable preview, saved versions, and PDF output.

## Content Integrity

- One degree appears once. The same Imperial College London Design Engineering degree must not become both an `Education` section and a second standalone Imperial section.
- One job, project, award, or achievement appears once unless two entries describe genuinely different work.
- Duplicate detection runs after import cleanup and after accepted AI changes. The longer supported version is retained and its evidence links are combined.
- AI changes must cite stored evidence. Unsupported experience, tools, metrics, dates, education, and achievements are rejected.

## One-Page Target

- A graduate CV should normally contain 450 to 650 words.
- CareerOS renders fixed A4 sheets. Content that does not fit the first sheet continues on a second A4 sheet instead of making one infinitely long page.
- Page packing uses measured rendered entry heights. Removing, shortening, tightening, or reordering content must pull later entries upward whenever genuine A4 space is available.
- Page count is shown explicitly. Two pages are permitted when the user chooses to retain enough relevant evidence to require them.
- The screen preview and print layout use the same page proportions and section order.

## Section Hierarchy

- Name, professional headline, email, phone, and website appear only on page one. The name may be centred with contacts below or left-aligned with contacts on the right.
- Section headings describe groups such as `Education`, `Professional Experience`, `Leadership & Activities`, `Projects`, and `Awards & Achievements`.
- A company, institution, project, or activity is an entry inside one of those groups. It must not become its own top-level section heading.
- Each entry keeps the organisation or project name on the left and its date on the right. The role, degree, or project type sits beneath it, followed by concise bullet points.
- Technical skills use compact category lines, for example `Programming: Python, TypeScript; Engineering: SolidWorks, Rhino`.
- Interests use one compact final line, for example `Interests: Piano, tennis, filmmaking, creative writing`.
- Skills and interests must not consume the same vertical space as a job, degree, or major project.
- Weak or irrelevant entries should be removed before text is compressed or made smaller.

## Typography

- CV body text must remain readable at normal zoom and print at approximately 10 to 11 points.
- Application Studio provides explicit font, body-size, line-height, section-spacing, and name-alignment controls. These settings are stored with each saved CV version.
- The global section gap may be reduced to zero, and each group heading has its own top-gap override.
- Group headings and individual entries can be dragged to change their order. Empty entries can be deleted and must not reserve invisible page space.
- CV headings use weight and spacing rather than oversized type.
- Application Studio side panels use normal product-interface body sizes. Dense information may wrap, but must not rely on 7 to 9 pixel text.
- Long evidence and before/after text remain readable through scrolling and progressive disclosure.
